## The chain

Each sentence picks up the thing the last one ended on:

click → wave → slices → one slice → pitch → slices side by side → picture → stripes → pictures → pattern → key

The sentence openers do the steering: "Each…", "Then, you…", "For each…", "Finally, you…", "As you can see…",
"So you…", "The catch is…".

## The look

- **Two worlds.** A warm 3D desk (dark wood, lamp, shallow focus, motion-blurred whips) for the claim, the proof and
  the close. A dark chalkboard with lines that draw themselves for the mechanism.
- **Four colours on the board.** Orange is E, blue is R, yellow is labels and the highlighted slice, green is "right".
- **Something new draws about every half second**, and each drawing is done when its sentence ends.
- **Breadcrumbs.** At 0:28 and 0:32 the earlier drawing shrinks into a small row at the top (key, wave, slices) and
  stays there.
- **Captions.** Small white sans, 2 to 4 words, a little below the middle, no highlight and no movement.
- **Layout.** Drawings in the top 60%; the lower third is empty.
- **The proof foreshadows the mechanism.** The phone screen at 0:05 already shows clicks → pictures → guess, which the
  board then explains.

## Why it got the engagement

Measured:

1. **The comments are the keyword.** One comment per nine likes is far above normal, and almost every readable
   comment is just the word.

A reading, not a measurement:

2. **A personal threat.** "Your password" on "a video call" is everyone's day, and "do not type passwords on a call"
   is a reason to send it to someone.
3. **The claim shows its own proof.** By the first second the password is being read off the screen. No intro, no face.
4. **Proof before explanation.** A named university and one number by 0:12.
5. **"How you'd build one."** The viewer is treated as a builder, which gives a reason to comment and save.
6. **The catch.** Admitting the limit makes the rest believable.
7. **No wasted second.** 15 sentences, each with one job, and the script alone carries it: there is no music.
